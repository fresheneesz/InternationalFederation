# International Law

1. Recognized nations may not prevent representatives in the international congress from attending the congress**
2. **Recognized nations must keep a military force**
    The force must be at least 1/10,000th of the country’s population unless fewer than that number of the country’s citizens are willing to participate in a military force.
3. **The military forces of international entities must be limited**
    Neither the number of military personnel per capita in active duty nor military funding per capita may exceed twice the mean average among all international entities.
4. **Recognized nations must aid the IF in the capture of uncooperative officials
   **Recognized nations are required to supply a military force of up to 1/10,000th that country’s population for use by the IF in capturing officials charged with international crimes who have failed to cooperate with trial proceedings. A nation is exempt from this requirement only if there is another nation that has not supplied its military forces since the first nation has last supplied its forces, and if that other nation is not being asked for its forces for the current need.  
5. **International entities must adhere to the rule of law**
    The law of that international entity must be written andd accessible to, and must be applied equally to all people in the relevant jurisdiction.  
6. **Right of the people to choose their government**
   Every 50 years, a ballot is held by the IF to determine if the people of each nation approve of their form of government or if they want to change it. If they do want to change it, a number of representatives are elected to discuss how to restructure their government. 
    **Rationale:** In cases where people are somehow prevented from bringing a trial to their government’s leaders, or in cases where the current international laws aren’t enough. People should be able to decide if they are happy with their form of government.
7. **Whistle blowers will be protected.** 
    No person may be held legally liable for releasing information reasonably necessary to prove that an international entity was breaking the laws of the IF or their own jurisdiction.
